## Top picks

### 1. [Expanding the Cyber Verification Program](https://www.anthropic.com/news/cyber-verification-program)
**Anthropic (no named authors) · Oct 6, 2026 (pre-window)**

Anthropic is expanding its Cyber Verification Program into three access tiers (Defense, Red Team, Specialized) for vetted security professionals, merged with Project Glasswing. The accompanying CyScenarioBench test of Claude Opus 5.5 (50 trials per tier) shows Defense Access blocking 46 trials. Red Team Access had no blocks and completed 34 of 50 tasks, against a 67.6% success rate with no safeguards. The post also claims Glasswing partners found at least 129,000 verified vulnerabilities from April to July 2026, plus 5,500 from Anthropic's open-source scanning through October, with the true impact "likely at least five times higher". These are vendor-reported figures.

**Methodology:** Vendor policy announcement with a small-N (50 trials per tier) safeguard-impact evaluation on CyScenarioBench.

**Why it matters:** It quantifies how much classifier-based safeguards cost offensive-security task completion, and shows the access route researchers doing offensive-agent work would need.

### 2. [Anthropic Cyber Mission](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-cyber-mission)
**Anthropic (no named authors) · Oct 8, 2026 (pre-window)**

Announces the Cyber Mission, including a Critical Infrastructure Defense Program with eleven founding partners and free, opt-in OSS Scanner runs (the service covered in last week's digest). It states that reports are not human-reviewed and that Anthropic expects a true-positive rate above 90%. The central argument is that vulnerabilities are easier to find than to verify and fix, and it forecasts that AI will favor defense in about two years.

**Methodology:** Program announcement; claims rest on internal figures, not a published evaluation.

**Why it matters:** The "find is easier than verify and fix" framing is a research opening for validation and patching work, but the claims are not independently checkable.

## Also notable

### [An update on independent evaluators](https://www.anthropic.com/news/an-update-on-independent-evaluators)
**Anthropic (no named authors) · Oct 15, 2026**
Anthropic has embedded a METR team (six employees with employee-like access) and is working with SecureBio, Accenture and Faculty under a three-tier access model. METR can publish its analysis, with Anthropic able to require redaction only of predefined sensitive categories. *Relevance:* only indirect, a governance model for third-party evaluation of frontier models' risk, including cyber risk.

### [Managing open-weight model risk in enterprise deployments](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/10/16/managing-open-weight-model-risk-in-enterprise-deployments/)
**Michael Scovetta (Microsoft) with NVIDIA contributors · Oct 16, 2026**
A joint Microsoft/NVIDIA paper and blog post with a five-category risk taxonomy (Cybersecurity, Data Protection, Content, Human-AI Interaction, Bias) that maps evaluations to risks. It notes that evaluating a model alone does not establish the safety of an agent, since tools, permissions and runtime also matter. *Relevance:* a threat-modeling vocabulary for agent deployments, with little offensive or code-security content.
